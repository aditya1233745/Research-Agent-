# Run: What caused the 2008 financial crisis?



## Final Answer

The 2008 financial crisis was primarily triggered by the collapse of the U.S. housing market and the widespread default on "subprime" mortgages. This triggered a domino effect that brought down major global financial institutions and led to the worst economic downturn since the Great Depression.

 Key Causes of the Crisis Subprime Lending: Banks gave high-risk mortgages to borrowers with poor credit histories. These loans often started with low introductory interest rates that later spiked, making them impossible for borrowers to repay.Predatory Lending Practices: Lenders often aggressive pushed these risky loans without verifying the buyers' actual income, betting that rising home prices would cover the risk.Asset Securitization: Financial institutions bundled thousands of these risky mortgages into complex financial products called Mortgage-Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Debt Obligations (CDOs).Flawed Credit Ratings: Rating agencies gave these toxic, mortgage-backed products "AAA" ratings (the highest and safest grade), misleading investors worldwide into thinking they were buying low-risk assets.Deregulated Financial Markets: Laws passed in the late 1990s and early 2000s allowed commercial banks to engage in risky investment practices. This lack of government oversight allowed banks to take on massive amounts of debt (leverage) to buy these toxic mortgage products.The Housing Bubble Burst: When U.S. interest rates rose and home prices finally began to fall around 2006, millions of homeowners defaulted on their loans. The value of MBS and CDOs collapsed, leaving global banks holding trillions of dollars in worthless assets.
